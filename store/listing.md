## Pricing

Apple allows any price from $0.29, in $0.10 steps up to $9.99, so $1.29 is a valid choice.

My suggestion: **launch at $0.99, then raise it step by step.**

1. **Launch: $0.99** for the first 2 to 4 weeks. $0.99 is the impulse price people already know, and the first ratings matter more than the extra 30 cents. $1.29 earns about $0.25 more per sale after Apple's cut, which is unlikely to make up for the buyers who stop at a number that isn't 99.
2. **After your first ratings (around 20 or more): $1.99.**
3. **After a content update (a new court or rival): $2.99.** Then run short drops back to $0.99 for promotions. Sites that track price drops only notice an app when its price falls from a higher one.

Apple sets matching local prices for every country automatically, and you can override any of them, Turkey included. Enroll in the **App Store Small Business Program** so Apple keeps 15% instead of 30%. At $0.99 that leaves you about $0.84 per sale before local taxes.

This is a recommendation, not financial advice. Your own sales numbers should drive the later steps.
